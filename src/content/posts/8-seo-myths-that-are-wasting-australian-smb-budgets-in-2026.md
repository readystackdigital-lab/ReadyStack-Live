Australian small businesses spend hundreds of millions on SEO every year, and a large share of it produces nothing measurable. The problem isn't that SEO doesn't work. The problem is that most of what SMBs are sold as SEO is either outdated, incomplete, or actively harmful.

We audit websites for Sydney professional services firms every week, and the same handful of myths keep costing owners real money, usually between $500 and $3,000 a month that could be doing something more useful.

Here are the eight most expensive ones.

---

## Myth 1: "You need to publish new content every week"

You've probably been pitched a content package: four blog posts a month, "SEO-optimised", for $800 to $1,500. The pitch is that Google rewards fresh content, so volume equals rankings.

It doesn't. Google rewards content that answers a query better than anything else on the internet, and that has almost nothing to do with how often you publish.

What actually happens with weekly content packages: a copywriter with no industry knowledge writes 800 words of generic filler, sprinkles in some keywords, and pushes it live. Six months later you have 24 posts, all indexed, none ranking. Worse, they signal to Google that your site is a low-quality content farm, dragging down the pages that could have ranked.

What to do instead: publish fewer, better pieces. One 2,500-word pillar guide targeting a real buyer question ("How much does a family lawyer cost in Sydney?") will out-earn a year of thin blog posts. Update it quarterly. That's it.

---

## Myth 2: "You need more backlinks, here's a link package for $299"

Backlinks still matter, but the market for cheap ones is a graveyard. Fiverr link packages, directory submissions, guest posts on "high DA" blogs no human reads: these were mildly effective in 2015 and are now either useless or actively penalised.

Google's link spam updates, running continuously since 2021, are specifically designed to devalue manipulated links. In severe cases, a cheap link campaign can trigger a manual penalty that takes six months and a disavow file to recover from.

What to do instead: earn a small number of genuinely relevant links. A single mention from a real Australian industry publication, a legitimate partner site, or a local news outlet is worth more than 500 directory listings. If you don't have time to earn them, don't buy them. Do nothing and focus on on-page fundamentals instead.

---

## Myth 3: "We guarantee page 1 on Google"

If an agency guarantees page 1 rankings, walk away. Google itself explicitly says no one can guarantee rankings, and any agency claiming otherwise is either dishonest or planning to rank you for a keyword nobody searches for.

The common bait-and-switch: they'll guarantee page 1 for "family lawyer North Sydney Chatswood professional services" (a 12-word phrase that gets zero real searches), then invoice you as if they'd ranked you for "Sydney family lawyer".

What to do instead: ask any prospective SEO agency two questions. First, what specific keywords will you target, and what's the monthly search volume for each? Second, what's your definition of success: traffic, leads, or something that can't be measured? Vague answers on either are a red flag.

---

## Myth 4: "SEO takes 12 months, just trust the process"

There's a kernel of truth here: SEO is slower than paid ads. But "trust the process" is often code for "I don't have anything to show you and I want to keep billing you $2,000 a month for another six".

Real SEO progress produces observable signals long before rankings move. Within 30 days you should see indexed pages increasing, crawl errors resolved, Core Web Vitals improving, and impressions climbing in Google Search Console. Within 90 days you should see click-through rates lifting on target queries. If none of that is happening, the work isn't working.

What to do instead: ask for monthly reporting on leading indicators, not just rankings. Impressions, CTR, indexed pages, Core Web Vitals scores, and technical audit fixes are all measurable within weeks. If your agency can't show progress on any of those in the first quarter, something is wrong.

---

## Myth 5: "Target the highest-volume keywords"

This one wastes more Australian SMB budget than any other. An accountant pays for six months of trying to rank for "accountant" (35,000 monthly Australian searches, dominated by MYOB, Xero, and the CPA), gets nowhere, and concludes SEO doesn't work.

Meanwhile, "small business accountant Parramatta" gets around 90 searches a month, has almost no serious competition, and every one of those searches is a person actively trying to hire an accountant.

What to do instead: target intent, not volume. For most Australian professional services SMBs, the right keywords are three or four words long, include a service or location specifier, and get 50 to 500 searches a month. A page ranking #1 for ten of those out-earns ranking #12 for one high-volume term every single time.

---

## Myth 6: "You need WordPress for SEO"

This one usually comes from agencies that only know WordPress. WordPress can rank fine, but it's not required, and it's rarely the fastest path to a technically clean site in 2026.

Google uses page experience as a ranking signal, and Core Web Vitals (Largest Contentful Paint, Interaction to Next Paint, Cumulative Layout Shift) are objectively measurable. A stock WordPress install with a page builder, five plugins, and shared hosting will fail Core Web Vitals almost every time. Fixing it means caching plugins, image optimisation plugins, database maintenance, and constant security patching.

Modern static site frameworks (Astro, Next.js, Eleventy) on edge hosting (Cloudflare Pages, Netlify) pass Core Web Vitals out of the box, cost less to host, and don't need weekly plugin updates.

What to do instead: don't switch stacks purely for SEO, but don't accept the claim that WordPress is required, either. If your current WordPress site is slow, invest in serious performance work or consider a static rebuild. For a 10 to 20 page professional services site, a full rebuild often costs less than 12 months of "SEO retainer".

## Myth 7: "Local SEO is just claiming your Google Business Profile"

Google Business Profile is table stakes, not a strategy. Claiming it, filling it out, and adding some photos will get you into the local pack for very low-competition queries. It won't move you much on anything competitive.

Real local SEO for Australian professional services needs: consistent NAP (name, address, phone) across your site, GBP listing, and any directory citations; LocalBusiness schema markup with geo coordinates; separate location pages if you serve multiple suburbs; and reviews on GBP with keyword-relevant response text. "Australia-wide" or "servicing all Sydney" in a footer is a negative signal. Google wants specificity.

What to do instead: anchor your site to a specific suburb. Include the address in an HTML `<address>` element in the footer. Add LocalBusiness schema with lat/long. If you serve multiple areas, build a dedicated page per area with genuinely different content, not the same page with the suburb name find-and-replaced.

## Myth 8: "AI is killing SEO, just wait it out"

The opposite. AI search is changing what "showing up in search" means, and Australian SMBs who wait it out will lose ground to businesses optimising for it now.

Google AI Overviews now appear in roughly 45% of searches and reduce click-through to websites by up to 58%. ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Claude are increasingly used for local business queries like "best conveyancer in Newcastle", "family lawyer Northern Beaches", or "IT support Parramatta". These systems cite specific businesses in their answers. If your site isn't structured for extraction, you don't get cited.

Getting cited by AI systems requires a specific technical foundation: an `llms.txt` file summarising what your business does; structured data (Organization, LocalBusiness, ProfessionalService, FAQPage schemas); content organised as clear question-answer blocks; and a `robots.txt` that explicitly allows AI crawlers such as GPTBot, ChatGPT-User, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended, and Applebot-Extended.

One trap specific to Cloudflare-hosted sites: Cloudflare's default settings block AI training bots and can override your site's robots.txt. If you're on Cloudflare, check that "Manage your robots.txt" is set to "Do not manage" and "Block AI training bots" is set to "Do not block". Otherwise you may be invisible to AI search without knowing it.

What to do instead: treat AI visibility as a distinct workstream. It uses the same technical foundation as traditional SEO (fast pages, clean HTML, structured data) but adds its own requirements. Optimising for AI search now, while most of your competitors don't, is the closest thing to a free lunch in Australian SMB marketing this year.

## What actually moves the needle

Strip away the myths and the fundamentals are surprisingly boring:

- A fast, technically clean site: passes Core Web Vitals, valid HTML, working sitemap, correct robots.txt, HTTPS, no broken links, indexable pages.
- Structured data: Organization, LocalBusiness, ProfessionalService, FAQPage where relevant. One-time setup, indefinite payoff.
- A handful of well-targeted service pages: one page per service, each answering the questions a real buyer would ask, each targeting a specific intent-driven keyword.
- A small number of long, genuinely useful pillar pieces: written by someone who knows the industry, updated quarterly, with real examples.
- A properly configured Google Business Profile: consistent NAP, categories set correctly, photos, reviews, service areas defined.
- An AI-visibility layer: `llms.txt`, correct `robots.txt`, structured content, Cloudflare settings sanity-checked.

That's most of what matters. You don't need weekly blog posts, a link-building package, or a $2,000-a-month retainer.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much should an Australian SMB spend on SEO?

For most professional services SMBs, effective SEO is a $2,000 to $6,000 upfront investment in the technical foundation, plus $300 to $800 a month for ongoing content and monitoring. Retainers above that are usually justified only for competitive verticals or aggressive growth targets.

### How long before SEO produces leads?

For a new site targeting realistic keywords, technical fixes and indexing improvements show up in Search Console within 30 days, meaningful traffic increases within 90 days, and organic lead volume typically stabilises around six months in.

### Is SEO worth it if AI is answering questions directly?

Yes, but the game has changed. Traditional SEO still drives traffic for high-intent queries where buyers compare and choose. AI SEO, which is optimising to be cited by ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews, is a new discipline Australian SMBs should be adding, not replacing SEO with.

### Can I do SEO myself?

The technical foundation (schema markup, `llms.txt`, `robots.txt`, sitemap, Core Web Vitals) is a one-time job that's easier to outsource. Ongoing content, Google Business Profile management, and review responses are things a business owner can do well without an agency.

### What's the single biggest SEO mistake Australian SMBs make?

Paying for volume: content volume, link volume, keyword volume, instead of paying for precision. A small, well-targeted, technically clean site beats a big generic one every time.
